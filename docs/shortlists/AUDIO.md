## 3. Packs worth taking in one go

1. **Medieval sound effects - Weapon impacts (MWI)**: one CC0 download (two .7z archives, about 156 MB) covering clang, block, hit, tell, projectile deflect. Cohesive because it is one recording set. Covers 4-5 of the nine sounds.
2. **Kenney set (RPG Audio + Casino Audio + Interface Sounds + UI Audio + Music Jingles)**: all CC0, all one author, about 335 files. Covers UI, rune ticks, coins, doors, footsteps, the whole casino, and stingers.
3. **JC Sounds Fantasy SFX Vol 1** (58 files, 4.7 MB, **CC-BY 4.0**, credit "JC Sounds"): the only verified single pack with fire/ice/electric spells and bow audio together. Take it only if you accept one credit line; otherwise use lentikula (CC0) for spells and accept the bow gap.
4. Cheap extras: NOX (impacts), dhallcomposer (ambience), lentikula (spells), EZduzziteh (hurt).

Not verified: **Kenney Impact Sounds** (page blocked to my fetcher) and **Sonniss GameAudioGDC** (official pages would not load; see the surprise note below).

## 4. Technical notes

- **Short effects:** mono, 44.1 kHz (22.05 kHz is fine for ticks and rumble), 16-bit WAV, trimmed with a 5-10 ms fade, imported as QOA as now. Keeps them tiny and decodes cheaply. Sources are often 48 kHz/24-bit stereo, so convert and sum to mono.
- **Long ambience:** mono, 22.05 kHz, OGG Vorbis (about q3), 15-30 s, with the loop point set. Compressed, it costs a fraction of PCM, which matters on a WebKit tab. Using OGG means a small change to `sfx.gd`, which currently loops a WAV in code. Godot behaviour here is from my knowledge, not tested.
- Budget: aim for under about 1.5 MB of audio total, and keep only 2-3 beds loaded at once.

(3) Two Kenney packs have no contents listing on their pages; "CC0" is verified, the sound names are not.
